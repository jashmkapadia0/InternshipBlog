<section class="page-hero"><p class="eyebrow">01 / RESEARCH CONTEXT</p><h1>Imaging from sparse Fourier samples</h1><p class="lede">The inverse problem begins with how an interferometer samples the sky, not with a neural network.</p></section>
<section class="section prose-section">
<h2>Project abstract</h2><p>Radio interferometers measure sparse samples of the sky's spatial Fourier transform. Their incomplete UV coverage produces dirty images shaped by the point spread function, so reconstruction requires deconvolution or other prior information. This project documents a planned comparison of Högbom CLEAN and a U-Net based reconstruction approach for radio interferometric imaging. The comparison is intended to examine image fidelity, structural similarity, integrated flux recovery, and computational runtime under a common, documented evaluation protocol. Dataset, implementation, and experiment details will be added as they are established; no findings are claimed at this stage.</p><p class="small-note"><strong>Keywords:</strong> radio interferometry · UV coverage · visibility · PSF · Högbom CLEAN · U-Net · image reconstruction</p>

<h2>Radio interferometry</h2><p>An interferometer combines signals from pairs of antennas. After calibration and correlation, each baseline measures a complex visibility, conventionally written <i>V(u,v)</i>. In a simplified narrow-field model, visibility is a Fourier component of the sky brightness distribution. The coordinates <i>(u,v)</i> are the projected baseline components measured in wavelengths; direction cosines <i>(l,m)</i> describe position on the sky.</p>
<div class="equation">V(u,v) = ∫∫ A(l,m) I(l,m) e<sup>−2πi(ul+vm)</sup> dl dm</div><p class="small-note">Simplified measurement equation for a small field of view. A is the primary beam response and I is sky brightness. Practical imaging may also account for the w-term, calibration errors, weighting, and other instrumental effects.</p>
<h2>UV coverage and visibility measurements</h2><p>Earth rotation changes the projected baselines during an observation, tracing tracks through the UV plane. The resulting UV coverage is finite and nonuniform: an array samples only selected spatial frequencies. Each sampled complex visibility carries information about source structure at a corresponding angular scale. Missing frequencies and noise make image recovery underdetermined.</p>
<h2>Dirty image and PSF</h2><p>Taking an inverse Fourier transform of sampled, weighted visibilities produces the <strong>dirty image</strong>. In the image domain, it is approximately the true sky convolved with the <strong>point spread function (PSF)</strong>, also called the dirty beam. The PSF is the image-domain response associated with the sampling and weighting pattern. Sidelobes can make bright sources appear surrounded by structured artifacts.</p>
<h2>Högbom CLEAN</h2><p>Högbom CLEAN iteratively finds a peak in the residual image, subtracts a scaled and shifted PSF, and records a point-source component. Once a stopping criterion is met, the components are convolved with a clean beam and combined with the residual image. It is an established deconvolution approach; its assumptions, stopping rule, gain, and restoration choices need to be recorded for a meaningful comparison.</p>
<h2>Why examine a U-Net?</h2><p>A U-Net is an encoder–decoder convolutional network with skip connections between corresponding scales. It can learn a mapping from an input image representation—such as a dirty image—to a target image from training examples. Whether that mapping generalizes to unseen sources, noise levels, UV coverages, or instruments is an experimental question. The network does not remove the need to define the forward model or validate flux and structure.</p>
<h2>Scope and questions</h2><ol><li>How do CLEAN and a U-Net behave under the same measurement conditions and evaluation references?</li><li>How sensitive is reconstruction quality to UV coverage, noise, source morphology, and preprocessing?</li><li>What are the trade-offs among image quality, flux recovery, and runtime?</li></ol><p class="callout"><strong>Current status:</strong> This page establishes background and project scope. It does not assert that a dataset has been selected, a model has been trained, or a result has been obtained.</p>
